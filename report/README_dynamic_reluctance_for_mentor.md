## 1. What this increment is

The double auction of Kumar and Kumar has two private numbers on every hospital–cell link:

- **Preference** (buyer side): how much a healthcare service provider (HSP) wants uplink from a base station (BS). We already make this from LSTM forecasts of patient vitals (eICU Demo).
- **Reluctance** (seller side): how costly it is for that cell to sell extra capacity (load, poor radio link, backhaul, impact on ordinary users).

Until now, reluctance was a **constant of 1** on every link: every cell, every hospital, same selling cost. That makes the market one-sided: hospitals can shout louder for a cell that will have many critical patients, and the auction pushes traffic there even if the cell is already strained.

This increment makes reluctance **per link** and **tied to radio conditions**, without changing the auction rules (same OPT1/OPT2, same KKT loop, same economic properties *inside one slot*).

**Market size used here (paper notation):**  
**W = 2 HSPs** (HSP1 = emergency, HSP2 = cardiology)  
**K = 3 BSs** (BS1, BS2, BS3)

The earlier 3-HSP × 4-BS eICU figures are left as they were.

## 2. Where the numbers come from (important)

eICU has **vitals**, not 5G counters. So:

- **Preference** still comes from real ICU numerics (LSTM → criticality → ρ).
- **Reluctance radio features** are **simulated** on the same 2 km map where patients are associated to cells. Mean RSRP (signal strength) of each (HSP, BS) group is computed from that map. Cell load, backhaul occupancy, native-user QoS degradation, and neighbour interference are generated by a simple physics-inspired model.

We do **not** claim operator RAN traces. The honest sentence is: *dynamic preference from real ICU numerics; dynamic reluctance from a radio simulator on the same association geometry.*

**Features used for reluctance ω:**

1. Cell load
2. Mean link quality (from RSRP on the map)
3. Backhaul occupancy
4. Native-user QoS degradation
5. Inter-cell interference

Ground truth: a closed-form logistic map ω* = σ(aᵀξ + b).  
A small gradient-boosting model is then trained to recover ω* from **noisy** versions of those five features (what a BS could measure). Training uses **3,000 simulated slots** (18,000 link-rows with 3×2 links), split by slot so the evaluation map is not the training set.

Held-out test: MAE ≈ **0.019**, R² ≈ **0.97**.

## 3. What the auction did

Three one-slot clearings, same patients and same ρ:


| Setting      | Meaning                                             | Social welfare | Total rate | Market gap |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| ω = 1        | Old prototype (every cell equally reluctant)        | 20.14          | 42.0 Mbps  | 8.6×10⁻⁴   |
| Dynamic ω̂   | Predicted reluctance on the usual map (mean ≈ 0.50) | 22.66          | 61.8 Mbps  | 9.4×10⁻⁴   |
| Stressed BS1 | Same map, BS1 load/interference raised              | 21.96          | 54.9 Mbps  | 1.2×10⁻³   |


Reading of the numbers:

- The inner loop still **clears** (gap about 10⁻³). All HSPs keep positive surplus; all BSs keep positive profit.
- Typical cells are **less** costly than the old ω = 1, so the market sells more volume and welfare is higher. That is expected: unit reluctance was a harsh uniform cost.
- When **BS1 is congested**, its reluctance rises to about **0.85** (from about **0.40**). The rate **that cell sells falls** (emergency 5.9 → 3.7 Mbps; cardiology 14.0 → 9.3 Mbps). BS2 and BS3 do not lose volume. That is the point of seller-side information: a loaded cell bids higher and offers less, instead of absorbing all the extra healthcare demand.

Cardiology still gets more rate than emergency, because its preference ρ is larger (more criticality mass). Reluctance does not erase the clinical ranking; it only stops dumping extra load onto a strained cell.
